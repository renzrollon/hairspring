## How this skill applies the practice

- Uses a questionnaire where it is strong: confirming or quantifying findings from interviews and observation across many stakeholders who cannot all be interviewed.
- Ties every question to one hypothesis, so each answer distribution says something about exactly one finding.
- Fixes the analysis (metric, threshold, minimum number of answers) before fielding, so results cannot be reinterpreted to fit expectations; unknown cut-offs stay `TBD` and make the result inconclusive.
- Chooses closed questions when the options are known and open questions when the respondents' words matter, and pilots both before fielding.
- Feeds results back to the candidates and requirements they came from as evidence, without turning a survey result into a requirement by itself.

## What this skill deliberately leaves out

- Discovering needs nobody has voiced yet: `interview-guide`, `observation-protocol`.
- Coding large volumes of free-text answers into candidates: `artifact-mining` (feedback analysis).
- Phrasing supported findings as requirements: `requirement-phrasing`.
