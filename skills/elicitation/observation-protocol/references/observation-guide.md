# Observation guide

People describe their work as it should be. Watching them shows it as it is: the extra steps, the paper lists, the waiting, the workarounds that have become so normal nobody mentions them in an interview. Each workaround is evidence of an unmet need, and usually a dissatisfier: its absence would not be praised, its presence causes complaints.

## Choose the method

| Method | The observer | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Field observation | Watches and notes; asks only in pauses | Work is fast, customer-facing or must not be disturbed; you need many episodes | The work is mostly in people's heads (decisions, judgement) |
| Apprenticing | Does the work under the guidance of the person who normally does it | Work can be learned in hours, is safe for a novice, and relies on tacit knowledge | Mistakes harm customers, money or safety; the work needs certification |
| Mixed | Watches first, then tries a few tasks | Most real sessions | |

The observer changes what is observed: people work more carefully when watched. Plan for it: stay long enough for routine to return, and ask in the debrief "What would have been different if I hadn't been here?"

## Pick the moment

- Observe when the problem happens: a peak, a month-end, a release night. A quiet morning shows the process, not the pain.
- Write down why the slot was chosen and what it will not show ("evening peak; no morning deliveries").
- One session is one sample. Say so in the synthesis and suggest a second site or slot if the findings matter.

## What to look for

| Signal | Looks like | Often means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper or sticky notes | A list taped to the till, a notebook of numbers | Missing data or lookup in the system |
| Re-typing | Copying a number from one screen or device into another | Missing integration |
| Asking someone | "Can you check this for me?" | Missing permission, knowledge or information |
| Waiting | Staring at a spinner, queue building | Performance problem at a specific step |
| Repeating | Second and third scan attempts | Unreliable input or device |
| Overrides | Supervisor keys, manual discounts | A rule the system enforces that does not fit reality |
| "Just" and "always" | "I just write it down", "we always do this when..." | A workaround that has become routine |
| Avoidance | A feature never used, a step skipped | The feature does not fit the work |

## Capture rules

- One row per kind of episode: the same trigger, steps and outcome. Repeats raise the observed count; they do not get new rows.
- Write what you see, not what you conclude: "typed the member's phone number into the notepad", not "the system is bad".
- Separate frequency **observed** (count in this session, with the duration) from **reported** (what people say in the debrief, marked "reported").
- Quote people's words exactly when they explain something; mark the speaker by role, not by name.
- Never write customers' names, phone numbers, card or ID numbers, or anything on a screen that identifies a member. Mask as `[member]`, `[phone]`, `[card]` if the notes contain them.
- No photos or recordings unless the consent note covers them and the data protection owner agreed; never of customers or of screens with member data.

## Debrief

Same day, while memory is fresh, with each observed person (or the group). Ask open questions about what you saw, not about what you think the solution is.

| Purpose | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| Confirm the episode | "I saw you <do X> when <Y happened>. Can you tell me about that?" |
| Reason | "What would happen if you didn't do that?" |
| Frequency | "How often does that happen in a normal shift or week?" (record as reported) |
| Need | "If this worked the way you want, what would be different?" |
| Typicality | "Was today a normal day? What usually happens that didn't today?" |
| Observer effect | "Did you do anything differently because I was watching?" |
| Gaps | "What should I come back to see?" |

## From workaround to candidate

1. Name the need the workaround compensates for, in domain words: the paper list of member numbers means "cashiers can identify a member when the QR code cannot be scanned".
2. One candidate per need, even if several workarounds point to it; list all episode IDs in `Reason`.
3. Tag `Kano=dissatisfier` for workarounds people consider normal; `TBD` when the debrief shows nobody minds.
4. Put the observed and reported frequency in `Reason`; it is the evidence for prioritization.
5. `Disposition=confirm` with the process owner as `Owner`: the observed person shows the need, the owner decides it.
