## How this skill applies the practice

- Treats documents, systems in operation and user feedback as requirements sources in their own right, next to stakeholders.
- Separates what an artifact shows (behavior) from what anyone wants (intent): legacy behavior becomes a candidate that a person confirms, not a requirement by default.
- Keeps backward traceability from the first minute: every candidate carries its source ID, location and verbatim quote, and a script checks the quote is really there.
- Drops implementation details of the old solution so they do not constrain the new one, while keeping any quality need visible behind them.
- Uses aggregated feedback as evidence of a need (with counts), and leaves the decision on that need to its owner.

## What this skill deliberately leaves out

- Deciding which sources exist and which technique each gets: `source-inventory`.
- Classifying and phrasing candidates as requirements: `requirement-classifier`, `requirement-phrasing`.
- Asking owners to confirm: `clarifying-questions`, `interview-guide`.
