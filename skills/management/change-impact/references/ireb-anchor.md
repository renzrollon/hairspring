## How this skill applies the practice

- Treats every change to an agreed requirement as a request with a requester, a reason and a decision, so the register never changes silently.
- Uses the trace links to find what a change touches, and falls back to a search that is labeled as lower coverage, instead of relying on memory.
- Lets the process decide who decides: a change control board in plan-driven or contract-bound work, the product owner in iterative work. The skill reads this from the repository's process settings.
- Separates analysis from decision and decision from update. Requirement rows change only after an approved decision, with a new version and a link back to the change request.
- Keeps effort and risk relative and explicit about their assumptions, because a precise-looking estimate made without the team is worse than none.

## What this skill deliberately leaves out

- Resolving conflicts between stakeholder positions that a change exposes: `conflict-detector`, `conflict-to-decision`.
- Building and maintaining the trace matrix itself: `traceability-matrix`.
- Ranking the approved change against other backlog items: `prioritization`.
