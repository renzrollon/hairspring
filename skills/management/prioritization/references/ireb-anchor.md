## How this skill applies the practice

- Treats a prioritization as a small project with its own goal, criteria, participants, items and technique, decided in that order before anyone gives a score.
- Offers ad hoc techniques for quick, low-stakes cuts and analytical ones when several stakeholders must accept the result, and says which one was used and why.
- Fixes the weights before scoring and records who agreed them, so the result cannot be tuned afterwards.
- Leaves the arithmetic to a script and reports the result in bands with ties flagged, because ordinal judgments do not support decimal precision.
- Routes stakeholder disagreement about priority to conflict handling instead of averaging it, so the disagreement stays visible until someone decides.

## What this skill deliberately leaves out

- Resolving the conflicts it finds: `conflict-detector` and `conflict-to-decision`.
- Sizing a single change request: `change-impact`.
