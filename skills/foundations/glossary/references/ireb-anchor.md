## How this skill applies the practice

- Treats the glossary as the contract for words: one meaning per term, a named owner who decides it, and a source for every definition.
- Detects homonyms and synonyms from evidence in the team's own files rather than from opinion, so the discussion starts from where each sense is used.
- Splits a homonym into qualified terms instead of choosing one sense silently, and picks a preferred term for a synonym set with the words to avoid listed next to it.
- Keeps rules and requirements out of definitions, so a definition stays stable while the rules around it change.
- Starts early and grows with the project: every other skill reads the glossary and sends new words back to it.

## What this skill deliberately leaves out

- Modelling the relationships and attributes of business objects: `domain-model`.
- Eliciting the meaning of a term from a stakeholder: `clarifying-questions` via `re-interview`.
