## How this skill applies the practice

- Treats conflict identification as a deliberate comparison: rows are grouped by subject, then checked for five observable kinds (contradiction, overlap, inconsistent conditions, differing level of detail, priority disagreement), including requirement-versus-constraint pairs.
- Separates what was observed (the kind) from why it exists (the type), because the type, not the symptom, decides how the conflict can be resolved.
- Records each stakeholder's position with its source, so the later decision is about positions people actually hold, not positions the agent inferred.
- Recommends a resolution technique per type and routes relationship and structural conflicts to escalation, since they cannot be settled by changing requirement text.
- Detects only: it never edits a requirement or picks a winner, keeping identification and analysis apart from resolution.

## What this skill deliberately leaves out

- Running the resolution session and documenting the decision: `conflict-to-decision`.
- Checking the other quality criteria of the set: `requirements-review`.
- Resolving term clashes on their own: `glossary`.
