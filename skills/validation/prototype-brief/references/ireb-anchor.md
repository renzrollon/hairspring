## How this skill applies the practice

- Uses a prototype as a validation technique for named requirements: every hypothesis points at an R-ID, so the result changes the register rather than ending as an impression.
- Makes the choice between a throwaway (exploratory) and an evolutionary prototype explicit, because the two need different quality bars and the second must never happen by accident.
- Chooses the lowest fidelity (paper, mock-up, native) that can produce the evidence needed, and moves questions that need more fidelity to their own check.
- Fixes the thresholds and stop criteria before the first session, so the outcome (validated, changed, dropped, inconclusive) is decided by the evidence, not by hindsight.
- Feeds results back through the normal channels: rewrites through `requirement-phrasing`, status changes only after the decision-maker confirms.

## What this skill deliberately leaves out

- Designing or building the prototype itself.
- Reviewing the written requirements for defects: `requirements-review`.
- Generating new requirements ideas with stakeholders: `elicitation-workshop`.
